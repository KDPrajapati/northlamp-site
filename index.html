Targets from last year

Each salesperson's target is that person's own sales over the same dates last financial year, plus a percentage you set. Use one percentage for everyone, or give each person their own.

Incentive on money received

The incentive is a share of the money received, never the amount billed. You set the share for each person.

Contests

Pick any of four: most new clients, fastest collection, most of one product sold, most profitable. Contests run for a month or a quarter, with a rupee prize you type. Fastest collection reads today's balances against the last 365 days of sales. A leader is named once two salespeople have figures to compare.

The April increment

For each person with a target, the page shows the increment the year so far would earn if the year ended today. Inside a band it rises evenly; the top band reaches 15% at 130% of target.

Salespeople are the ledger groups your clients sit under today. A client's sales, last year's too, count for the person whose group it sits under now. When a client is linked to more than one person, you pick who gets it. Targets count sales before GST. When a salesperson's first sale falls partway through last year's dates, last year is scaled up to the full period, and the page says so. Under 3 months is too new for a target.

What you see

The Today page lists what needs you, then the rest to act on. Across the top: Money, Clients, Inventory, Prices and, on Enterprise and above, Team.

  • MoneyWho owes you, how old the unpaid invoices are, and whom to call first.
  • ClientsWho buys from you, and how much.
  • InventoryWhat is short and the last day to order it, what is sitting dead, and the stock on a date you pick.
  • PricesSelling price and cost.
  • TeamWho sells, and how much. Enterprise and above.

Money, Clients, Inventory and Prices are each a set of cards. A card gives one figure in one sentence, or says what it still needs, such as a bank statement.

How it treats your books

  • It runs on your own PC. Your sales, clients, prices and stock live in a file on that PC.
  • It never writes to your accounting software. Every request it sends there is a read, the same kind made when you print a report.
  • Nothing from your books leaves your PC unless you use a feature that sends it, such as off-site backup to a cloud you choose, the optional AI assistant, sharing with your other laptops, or web search. WhatsApp order collection only reads orders in.
  • On a paid plan or a trial the internet features stay shut until you switch them on. On the Free plan, installed for one user, they normally start on. You can shut them under Settings, Internet access. The WhatsApp message buttons are on for every plan; they only open a chat you send yourself.
  • Off-site backup runs only after you choose a folder or storage address, and stops when you remove it. Sharing runs only after you choose a folder and start it.
  • Apart from features you set up, it sends on its own only a weekly version check and, once a licence key is in (not a trial), a licence check-in: once a day, and every 10 minutes on the main PC or any PC not approved. None of these carries figures from your books. The app's About page describes the version check and the daily check-in, and Airplane mode blocks them all.
  • After every live read it checks its figures against your accounting software's own totals, and Today's full list of things to act on says in one line which match, which do not and which were not checked.

A lamp for the people who run a business.

The problem

Every business keeps books. Few of the people who run one get to read them. The answers sit in the accounting software, where owners are rightly careful about touching anything, so they ask their teams instead and wait.

The product

NORTHLAMP runs on the owner's Windows PC, leaves the books where they are, and never writes to the accounting software. It brings the answers together in one app: what is owed, who is slipping, what is short, what each sale earned. The owner gets the answers without asking the team.

Where it stands

  • It runs on 64-bit Windows only. Each release carries a signed manifest, and when the app updates itself, it refuses any download that does not match it.
  • In use at an early customer.
  • Four plans: Free, Pro, Enterprise and Tycoon.
  • After every live read it checks its figures against the accounting software's own totals.

How it started

Krupesh Prajapati built NORTHLAMP for his own trading businesses. Every day he was asking three teams the same questions: accounts for what was owed, marketing for what was selling, dispatch for what had left. He tracked the figures first in a spreadsheet, then in live sheets with scripts, then built a version that read straight from the accounting software. An early customer saw it and wanted it too. He builds it with the help of AI tools.
